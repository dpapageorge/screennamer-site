Three keys, then it's gone

macOS keeps taking the screenshots. ScreenNamer adds one step to the capture you already do — it never replaces the built-in shortcuts.

Capture

Use ⌘⇧4, ⌘⇧3, or any shortcut you already have. Nothing changes.

Name

A small panel appears with the field already focused and a preview of the shot.

Return

The file is renamed in place and the panel disappears. Escape moves it to the Trash instead.

What it does

One job, done carefully.

Suggests names you've used

Start typing and earlier names appear, ranked by how recently you used them and how closely they match. Choosing one fills the field — it is never saved without you pressing Return.

Keeps up with rapid captures

Take five screenshots in a row and they queue behind one panel instead of stacking windows. Name them one at a time, or skip through.

Preserves your extension

Type a name, not a filename. PNG stays PNG. Duplicate names get a number instead of an error.

Discards the ones you don't want

Escape moves a screenshot to the Trash — recoverable from Finder, never silently deleted.

Lives in the menu bar

No Dock icon, no window taking up space. It is invisible until a screenshot lands.

Watches one folder, and only that one

You pick it. macOS remembers the grant. ScreenNamer never asks for access to the rest of your disk.

Nothing leaves your Mac. It can't.

ScreenNamer is sandboxed by macOS and requests no network access at all — not for analytics, not for updates, not for anything. The operating system would refuse the connection even if the app tried to make one.

There are no accounts, no trackers, and no third-party services. The names you type and the folder you choose stay on your machine.

Questions

Does it change my screenshot shortcuts?

No. ⌘⇧3, ⌘⇧4 and ⌘⇧5 keep working exactly as they do now. ScreenNamer watches the folder macOS saves to and reacts after the fact — it never intercepts the capture itself.

The panel takes a few seconds to appear. Why?

That is macOS, not ScreenNamer. While the floating thumbnail is showing in the corner of your screen, macOS waits about five seconds before writing the file to disk — so nothing can see it until then. Press ⌘⇧5, click Options, and turn off Show Floating Thumbnail, and screenshots save instantly. ScreenNamer mentions this the first time you open it.

Which folder does it watch?

Whichever one you pick during setup — usually your Desktop, or wherever you pointed macOS with defaults write com.apple.screencapture location. It watches that folder and nothing else.

What happens if I press Escape by accident?

The screenshot moves to the Trash, where you can put it back with ⌘Z in Finder or by dragging it out. It is never deleted outright, precisely because a single keystroke shouldn't be able to lose your work.

Can I stop it temporarily?

Quit it from the menu bar. Renaming is what the app is, so there is no half-on state to get confused by — and Launch at Login decides whether it comes back tomorrow.

Does it use AI to name my screenshots?

No. Suggestions come from names you have typed before, on your own machine. Nothing is analysed, uploaded, or generated. You always write the name yourself.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple silicon and Intel.
