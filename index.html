Small details that keep ScreenNamer fast, predictable, and out of your way.

Remembers your names

Type a few letters and previous names appear, with the best match first. Nothing changes until you press Return.

Keeps up

Take as many screenshots as you need. They queue behind one panel instead of stacking windows. Name them one at a time.

Easy batch naming

Give multiple screenshots the same name and ScreenNamer numbers them automatically. File extensions stay untouched.

Trash, not delete

Press Esc to move a screenshot to the Trash. It stays recoverable until you empty it.

Questions

Does it change my screenshot shortcuts?

No. ⇧⌘3, ⇧⌘4 and ⇧⌘5 keep working exactly as they do now. ScreenNamer watches the folder macOS saves to and reacts after the fact. It never intercepts the capture itself.

Why does the panel sometimes take a few seconds to appear?

That is macOS, not ScreenNamer. While the floating thumbnail is showing in the corner of your screen, macOS waits about five seconds before writing the file to disk, so nothing can see it until then. Press ⇧⌘5, click Options, and turn off Show Floating Thumbnail, and screenshots save instantly. ScreenNamer mentions this the first time you open it.

I took a screenshot and nothing happened.

Almost always, the two folders don’t match. ScreenNamer is watching one folder while macOS is saving to another, so nothing ever arrives for it to rename.

Check them against each other. In ScreenNamer, open Settings and read the path under Folder to watch. Then press ⇧⌘5, click Options, and see which folder is ticked under Save to. Those two need to be the same folder. If the one you want isn’t in the list, choose Other Location… and pick it there.

Those are Step 1 and Step 2 of the Setup Guide, in the ScreenNamer menu. It ends with a test screenshot, so you can watch the two line up before you close it.

If ScreenNamer was working and then stopped responding, restart your Mac and it will pick up again. An update that fixes this is on its way.

Will it rename other images I save to that folder?

No. ScreenNamer only reacts to files macOS captured itself. Export a PNG from another app into the same folder, or drag one in, and it stays exactly as you left it, under the name you gave it.

macOS marks its own captures with a piece of file metadata, and that is what ScreenNamer looks for. The mark belongs to the file rather than its name, so a screenshot you renamed months ago still counts as one.

If you’d rather be prompted for everything that lands in the folder, open Settings and turn off Only rename screenshots.

Does it use AI?

No. And that’s intentional.

ScreenNamer remembers names you’ve used before. Nothing is analyzed, uploaded, or generated. You name your screenshots. ScreenNamer just makes it fast.

Which Macs does it work on?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple silicon and Intel.
